# Define the scope

Before planning, agree with the author on what the guide covers. Ask in rounds, never one long form.

## Format

Ask every question whose prerequisites are settled in the same round, numbered, each with your recommended answer. Wait for the answers, then ask the next round. Skip any question already answered by the author's first message, their material (slides, notes, a group survey) or an earlier round. Talk in the author's language.

```
❓ Q1 - [Short title]: [the question, with the choices if there are any]

➡️ [Your recommendation and why, in one sentence]
```

When the author answers «as you recommend», «you pick» or similar, take the recommendation. Push back once if an answer makes the guide hard to follow alone (for example, three hours of content for a one-hour session), then accept the author's call.

## The questions, in order

1. **Audience** — Who follows the guide, and do they follow it alone at home or in class with the author nearby? Recommend «alone at home»: every step then has a screenshot and its *If it fails*, which also helps in class. Describe the audience by what they want to achieve.
2. **Levels** — Is the group even, or mixed? Only if mixed, offer to segment explanations by level (`--levels`), with names that fit the topic, not necessarily tech names.
3. **Time and priority** — How long is the session, and what is essential versus what can be finished at home? Recommend ordering the big parts so each one is complete on its own; whatever does not fit becomes challenges for home.
4. **Starting point** — Do they start from nothing, or from something the author hands them (a file, a kit, a template)?
5. **What they need** — Accounts, programs, materials or equipment, and what must be ready before the session. Recommend creating accounts at home, before the session.
6. **The author's accounts** — May you use the author's accounts to create real things for the example (a project, a database, a deploy)? Never use them without a yes.
7. **Screenshots** — May you use a browser to take the screenshots of the resources and of what you build? Recommend yes. If yes, follow `screenshots.md` to choose the tool; never ask the author to know what Playwright is.
8. **Earlier guides** — Has the group already done other guides you should link instead of repeating steps? Ask for their links.
9. **Material** — Slides, notes, a script or a short survey of the group, if the author has them. If they share survey answers, ask for them without names or contact details.
10. **Check questions** — Add a few optional check questions? Recommend «yes, a few»: at most one per step, only where something must be understood.
11. **Language and look** — Confirm the guide's language (the one you are talking in) and say it will use the default red and black theme. Offer to use the author's color (and logo) instead.

Do not ask about challenges: propose them only when question 3 left something for home. Do not ask about the rescue prompt: it goes in every guide where the reader uses an AI agent.

## Closing the scope

When every question is settled, show a short summary table (decision per topic) and ask the author to confirm. Save it as the «Decisions» section of `PLAN.md`.
